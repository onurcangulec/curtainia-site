Sheer, voile or net: choosing a sheer curtain by its weight

Sheers are sold by name, but they behave according to their weight. What 25, 60, 100 and 180 g/m² actually look like at a window, and which to choose for privacy or light.

"Sheer", "voile" and "net" are used loosely, and two shops will use them for the same cloth. A more useful way to choose is by weight, measured in grams per square metre (g/m²). Weight tells you how transparent a sheer is, how it hangs and how well it holds a pleat.

What the weights look like

  • Under about 60 g/m². Net weight. Very transparent, almost no body. It diffuses daylight and takes the hard edge off a view, but gives little privacy.
  • About 60–110 g/m². Classic voile. Softens the light, blurs the view in during the day, and gathers into soft even folds. The range most rooms choose.
  • Above about 110 g/m². Heavy sheer. Hangs with real weight, holds a pinch pleat or ripple fold crisply, and gives noticeably more daytime privacy. Some are textured or slubbed and read almost like a light drape.

Privacy: by day and by night

Any sheer gives more privacy by day than by night. In daylight the outside is brighter than the room, so people see the cloth, not you. After dark, with lights on inside, the effect reverses and even a heavy sheer becomes see-through. If night-time privacy matters, pair the sheer with a drape or a roller blind.

Fullness and heading

Sheers need generosity. At 2× fullness a voile looks right; at 2.5× to 3× it looks luxurious, and because the cloth is light the stack stays small. Ripple fold shows off an even wave; a double pinch pleat looks tailored; a flat panel is for a sheer meant to read as a single plane.

Short answers

What is the difference between voile and sheer?

The names overlap. Voile usually means a plain, fine sheer of roughly 60–110 g/m². Weight is the more reliable guide to how transparent a sheer is and how it hangs.

Do sheer curtains give privacy at night?

Not on their own. With lights on inside, sheers become see-through after dark. Pair them with a drape or a blind for night-time privacy.
