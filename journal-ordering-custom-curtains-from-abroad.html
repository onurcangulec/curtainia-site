Ordering custom curtains from abroad: a checklist

Buying made-to-measure curtains from another country can save money or cause a month of trouble. Eight things to confirm in writing before you pay.

A made-to-measure curtain cannot be put back on a shelf, and one that crosses a border adds customs to the list of things that can go wrong. None of it is difficult if it is settled before you pay.

Before you order

  1. See the cloth first. Ask for samples. Colour on a screen is not colour in your room. A seller confident in the fabric will post them.
  2. Get the full landed price. Goods, making, freight, duty and tax. Ask whether the price is delivered duty paid. If duty is "payable by the customer", you do not yet know what the order costs.
  3. Know who the seller is. A company name, a registration number and a country you can check.
  4. Agree the measurements in writing. Which is the width, which is the drop, in which unit, measured from where. Most mistakes are unit or reference mistakes, not sewing mistakes.
  5. Confirm the fullness and heading. "A pair for a 72 inch window" means nothing until the fullness is stated.
  6. Ask for a making time and a shipping method. A date in writing, and a tracked door-to-door service.
  7. Read the remedy. Made-to-measure goods are normally excluded from change-of-mind returns everywhere. What matters is what the seller does if the curtain is not what was agreed.
  8. Check there is no minimum. Some workshops only take multi-window orders.

What a good quote looks like

One page: each window listed with its width, drop, cloth, heading, fullness and lining; one total in your currency with duty and delivery stated as included; a making time; the seller's company details. If any of those is missing, ask.

Short answers

Is it safe to order custom curtains from another country?

Yes, if you confirm in writing the landed price including duty, the measurements and units, the fullness and heading, the making time, and the seller's registered company details.

Can made-to-measure curtains be returned?

Usually not for change of mind, because they are made to the buyer's specification. A seller should still put right anything that was not made as agreed.
