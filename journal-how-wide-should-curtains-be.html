How wide should curtains be? Fullness, with a worked example

The most common reason a custom curtain disappoints is too little cloth across the window. What fullness is, which ratio to choose, and the arithmetic for a real window.

A curtain that is exactly as wide as the window is a flat sheet when closed. The folds that make a curtain look like a curtain come from extra cloth, and the amount of extra is called fullness.

Fullness as a ratio

Fullness is the width of cloth divided by the width of the track or pole it covers.

  • 1.5× — light. Flatter and more relaxed; useful when a bold pattern should stay readable.
  • 2× — standard. Full folds without bulk. Right for most rooms and most cloth.
  • 2.5× — full. Noticeably richer; our usual suggestion for a plain drape.
  • 3× — very full. Best kept to sheers and light cloth, or the stack at the side becomes wide.

A worked example

Take a window with a 72 inch (183 cm) pole and a pair of curtains at 2× fullness.

  • Cloth across the window: 72 × 2 = 144 inches (366 cm).
  • As a pair, each curtain is made from 72 inches (183 cm) of cloth, plus side hems.
  • Closed, the pair covers the 72 inch pole in full folds. Open, each curtain stacks back to roughly a quarter to a third of its covered width, depending on the heading and the weight of the cloth.

Because the curtains need somewhere to stack when open, the pole should run about 6–10 inches (15–25 cm) past the frame on each side. Measure the pole, not the glass.

Short answers

What does 2x fullness mean for curtains?

The curtain is made from twice as much cloth as the width it covers. A 72 inch pole at 2× fullness uses 144 inches of cloth across the window.

Is 1.5x fullness enough?

It gives a flatter, relaxed look and works for patterned cloth. For plain fabric it can look thin when closed; 2× is the safer standard.
