No minimum order: why one window is enough

Many workshops only take large orders. What a minimum order is really protecting, and why we price a single curtain on the same basis as a hundred.

Minimum order quantities are normal in textiles. A mill will not weave ten metres; a factory set up for volume loses money stopping a line for one pair. Buyers with a single window, a small apartment or a sample room for a larger project are often turned away or quoted a price meant to make them leave.

What a minimum protects

Usually it protects set-up cost: fabric bought by the roll, a line configured for one specification. If every curtain is cut individually to order, that cost is not there, and the minimum is a habit, not a necessity.

How we work instead

Every Curtainia curtain is cut for one named window, to order. Making one pair and making fifty are the same operation repeated, so we price them the same way.

  • Private customers: one window is an order.
  • Designers: a single room, a show flat, or one curtain to prove a specification before the client commits.
  • Hotels and operators: a pilot room first, then the rest in phases.
  • Replacements: one more curtain to match an earlier order, without re-ordering the set.

What does scale change?

Freight. A larger consignment costs less per curtain to ship than a single parcel, and we show that in the quote. The making price per window does not depend on how many windows there are.

The same applies everywhere we ship: the United States, delivered duty paid; the EU and the UK; and worldwide. Send us one window and see.

Short answers

Is there a minimum order for custom curtains at Curtainia?

No. One window is an order, for private customers, designers and hospitality projects alike.

Can I order a single curtain to test before a larger project?

Yes. A pilot room or single curtain is made and priced on the same basis as the full order, and the specification is kept on file for the rest.
