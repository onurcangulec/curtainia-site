Roller blinds

When a curtain is the wrong answer.

Kitchens, bathrooms, a window behind a worktop, a door that has to clear underneath. A roller blind sits inside the reveal, takes up nothing, wipes clean, and stops being in the way. We cut them to the millimetre, same as the curtains.

Two cloths, two jobs

You are really choosing between keeping light out and softening it.

Blackout

A coated cloth that stops effectively all the light through the fabric itself. Bedrooms, nurseries, a room with a screen in it. Light will still find the gap at the edge of the reveal — that is physics, not a fault — so for a truly dark room we fit outside the reveal and overlap.

Panama

A flat, even weave that filters rather than blocks. The room stays bright, the glare goes, and you keep a soft view of the outside. Matt, washable, no hem to collect damp — which is why it is the usual answer in a bathroom.

What we need from you

Four numbers and one decision.

01

Width

Measure the reveal in three places and give us the smallest.

02

Drop

From where the bracket will sit to where you want the blind to stop.

03

Inside or outside

Inside the reveal looks neater. Outside blocks more light.

04

Chain side

Left or right. Think about which side the sink or the door is on.
